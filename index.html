The problem and the person

Our target

Our fictional target values clear ingredient information and a calm evening routine. We assume she scrolls short video at night and checks a product page before buying; these are hypotheses, not observed customer data.

Why this idea fits

The proposed target values clear ingredients. We therefore connect the five-plant idea to a calm night ritual and use short video to introduce it. Search and the detail page would support an interested shopper who wants more information. These are strategic choices to test.

My advertising decisions

Stop. Feel. Remember.

Attention

The opening still contrasts a lit bottle and plants with a dark setting. I intend that contrast to catch a night-time scroller, but the quiet pace could also be easy to skip. I would test the opening rather than claim it wins attention.

Emotion

The ritual imagery and calm pace are intended to feel reassuring. That is our creative intention; it is not a measured emotional response or evidence of skincare efficacy.

Memory

The bottle label is visible in the opening; the final brand card repeats Cliffrose. Early identification matters because many viewers may leave before the ending. I would test whether viewers can name the brand after the opening and the full film.

What I revised, and why

Illustrative self-review: I separated the intended feeling from an observed result and labeled the KPI target as an assumption. This is a course example, not student feedback received from an instructor.

A second-market thought experiment · optional

Optional thought experiment: preserve the five-plant night-ritual promise, then check whether the plant imagery and wording carry that meaning for viewers in a second market. Ask actual viewers rather than infer preferences from nationality.

The test

How I would judge it

Measurement plan. Distinguish planned targets from any observed results and identify their evidence.

Attention-hold KPI: paid YouTube Shorts view rate (counted ad views divided by ad impressions). Before a test, record the current counted-view definition for this placement; do not substitute organic video starts.

Illustrative planning assumption: over 20% view rate over a two-week test. This is a proposed target, not a benchmark or observed result.

Basis and time window

Planning assumption, not a benchmark: 250,000 planned impressions at a 20% boundary would imply 50,000 counted ad views. $3,000 divided by 50,000 is $0.06 per view. This checks the arithmetic and budget implication; it does not establish that over 20% is achievable. Revise after a comparable test.

Where I would read it

Google Ads campaign reporting for the paid video placement. Check the metric definition for that placement before comparing numbers.

If it misses

If the view rate misses the target, examine the opening seconds and audience match, then test one changed opening against the original.

What this cannot prove

View rate is an attention-hold indicator. It does not show what viewers felt or understood, establish recall or purchases, or prove advertising caused an order. The understanding objective needs separate viewer evidence.

The recommendation

Shoppers do not yet know Cliffrose. The target hypothesis explains the ingredient story and the quiet creative. The hero and short film show how it could look. The media plan gives each placement a job. The KPI plan says what to check and what it cannot prove. I recommend approving a $5,000, two-week test of the night-ritual idea.
